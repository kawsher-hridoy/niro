## Recording rules

- Record **one slide per take**. Read the paragraph twice, put the script DOWN, record while looking at the screen/slide — talk to one imaginary friend.
- Keep small stumbles; they sound human. Re-record only if you lose the thread.
- **(…) = pause one full second.** Especially before punch lines. Don't rush them.
- **Bold** = punch the word harder.
- Do one full stopwatch dry-run before recording. If a slide runs long, cut words — never talk faster.
- Screen takes: pre-load every page. No live AI waits on camera except the ONE staged in Slide 4 (pre-warm it once so ~10 s is guaranteed).

## Script

### [1 · Cold open — the Bag] — 25 s · ≤54 words

*Visual: night. A table. A polythene bag tipped over — prescriptions and lab reports spill out. Hands pick one up; the handwriting is unreadable. No faces yet.*

It's two A.M. (…) This bag is my father's entire medical history. Heart disease. Diabetes. Years of paper. Somewhere in here is one answer I need: is he getting better (…) or worse? I can't find it. We're **Cortex Crew**. Stay till the end — this bag becomes **one living chart**. Watch.

### [2 · The problem] — 30 s · ≤65 words

*Visual: close-ups — crumpled reports, a pill strip, a glucometer. Overlay one stat card: "170,000,000 people · no medical record".*

You know this bag. (…) Open the cupboard at your home — it's there. In Bangladesh, this is the **only medical record** 170 million people have. Handwriting no one can read. Numbers no one explains. The doctor asks, "what was his sugar last year?" — and your mind goes blank. Tests get paid for **twice**. Slow dangers stay invisible. (…) So why hasn't an app fixed this?

### [3 · The failed fix] — 25 s · ≤54 words

*Visual: phone glowing in the dark — a chatbot with a report pasted in, a long English answer. A quiet red ✗ appears over it.*

People try chatbots. I did too. (…) Look — I paste one report, I get one answer. In English. Then it **forgets my father exists**. A chatbot reads a page. But health isn't a page. (…) Health is a **book**. So we built a reader for the whole book. It's called **Niro**.

### [4 · Demo A — one photo] — 35 s · ≤76 words

*Visual: screen recording, bright. Upload a prescription photo. Let the ~10 s AI wait play out — it proves it's real. The Bangla analysis renders. Cursor points at each medicine. Small English labels annotated in edit.*

Here's Niro, running live. I photograph this prescription — no typing, no prompt. (…) Ten seconds. (…) Done. Look at this: every medicine, the dose, the duration — explained in **plain Bangla**, the language my mother actually reads. Bangla numerals and all. The wall of numbers on a lab report? Same thing — each value explained, flagged if it's out of range. One photo — one **readable page**. (…) But where does that page **go**?

### [5 · Demo B — it files itself] — 30 s · ≤65 words

*Visual: the Records page (স্বাস্থ্য রেকর্ড). The new report appears under its category with its date. Scroll the category shelves: prescriptions, CBC, lipid profile.*

Watch the shelf. (…) Niro read that report, understood **what it is** — a blood-sugar test — pulled the date off the paper, and filed it in the right chapter. Automatically. Prescriptions here. CBC here. Lipid profiles here. Every page finds its chapter — the bag, organized while you sleep. (…) Chapters are neat. But the **plot** is where lives change.

### [6 · MONEY SHOT — the plot] — 45 s · ≤97 words

*Visual: Rahima's profile. Upload her newest report → it files itself → tap Trends (স্বাস্থ্য ট্রেন্ড) → the 13-month glucose chart draws: line, reference band, red abnormal points, the rising-trend insight. Hold on the chart. This is the thumbnail frame.*

Meet **Rahima** — and we're honest: she's a test patient we built, ten reports in real Bangladeshi lab formats, thirteen months of life. Here's the promise from the beginning. (…) I upload her newest report… it files itself… now — tap **Trends**. (…) There. Thirteen months of blood sugar. One line. Look at this red flag — values out of range. And Niro says it plainly: "**fasting glucose rising** over six months." Twenty-eight metrics get this — sugar, creatinine, hemoglobin. A bag can't show you this. A chatbot can't remember this. (…) This is the chart we promised.

### [7 · When the AI is wrong] — 30 s · ≤65 words

*Visual: quick cuts — a low-confidence result showing the "consult a doctor" recommendation, the inline disclaimer, the audit-log screen scrolling.*

But this is medicine — so what happens when the AI is **wrong**? (…) We planned for it. Niro never diagnoses, never prescribes. Every answer carries a confidence score — when it's low, Niro **recommends a human doctor**, on screen. A safety filter blocks dosing language. Every AI call lands in an audit log no one can edit. (…) A reader tells the story. It never writes the ending.

### [8 · The doctor + the chamber] — 30 s · ≤65 words

*Visual: verification flow — request → doctor's review appears. Then the chamber QR scan and the consent screen with the expiry timer.*

And when you need a doctor? One tap. A **BMDC-licensed** doctor reviews the AI's reading — from 200 taka. The price of lunch — not two thousand. Why so cheap? Niro hands them the whole book, open to the right page — review takes **five minutes**, not thirty. In offline chambers: scan a QR, share for the visit, access **auto-expires**. You stay the owner.

### [9 · Callback + close] — 30 s · ≤65 words

*Visual: back to the night table — same bag, same papers, but calm now. The phone lies on top, showing Rahima's chart. Fade to the Niro logo + tagline.*

So. (…) Back to this table. Same bag. Same papers. (…) But look — the phone on top now holds the whole story: filed, charted, explained in Bangla. Built end-to-end by us — FastAPI, PostgreSQL, a web app that runs on **any phone**. No app store. Nearly zero cost. Same bag. (…) **Different ending.** Niro — আপনার স্বাস্থ্য, আপন হাতে. Your health, in your own hands. Thank you.
